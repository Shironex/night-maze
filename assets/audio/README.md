# Sounds and music

Own work. Most sounds, the music of a round and the second theme of the menu were generated
by me in ElevenLabs under my commercial plan, which gives me the rights to them. Three sounds
(`gate_bell.wav`, `intro_bell.wav`, `intro_wind.wav`) and the first theme of the menu are
computed by scripts. The program reads the files at start
(`src/game/NightMazeApp.cpp`, through `audio::AudioEngine::load`) and plays them when the rules
in `src/game/SoundCues.cpp` say so. That file also holds the table that gives every sound cue
its file name.

Until 2026-10-16 all twenty-three files were computed by `tools/make_sounds.py` from sine waves
and noise; twenty of them are recordings now (see "The sounds generated in ElevenLabs" below), and
the table and the next lines describe the computed versions, which the script still makes and git
history keeps. The sounds that happen in the maze (the bells, the lever, the
gate, the heartstone, the steps and the sounds of the shadow) share one computed reverb, a stone room, each with
its own amount of it. The switch of the lamp, the pulse, the breath, the crystal and the flask have none: they happen in the hand. The script uses only the standard library of Python (no
numpy, no other package). The files are 16 bit, 44 100 samples per second, mono except for the
crystal, the heartstone and the gate, which are stereo. The wind of the maze has half that sample rate: nothing
in it lies above 3 kHz, so the file is half the size and sounds the same. The twenty-one sounds
of a round are about 2.7 MB together (the wind of the maze is 0.44 MB of that, the five steps
0.2 MB, the alert and the banish of the shadow 0.13 MB, the bell of the gate 0.26 MB, the
heartstone 0.39 MB). The wind of the intro is as long as the intro itself (30 seconds) and is 2.6 MB alone.

The game mixes the sounds in three groups, each with a volume of its own on the settings screen:
the two winds are "ambient", the themes of the menu and the music of a round are "music",
everything else is "effects" (the table in `src/game/SoundCues.cpp` says which).

The peaks differ on purpose: they are the mix of the game. The crystal and the gate stand in
front, the heartstone just behind them, the lever a step behind, the switch of the lamp is small, and the pulse and the breath
are the quietest because they repeat. No file peaks above -3 dBFS. The steps of the player are
heard twice a second for a whole round and stand below the hum of the shadow, the steps of the
shadow below those, and the wind of the maze below every sound that tells the player something.
The alert of the shadow stands a little above its hum, because it is heard once and must not be
missed. The banish is as soft as the hum: it is good news. The bell of the gate repeats until
the round ends, so it stands below the one bell of the intro.

### The sounds generated in ElevenLabs

Twenty sounds were replaced on 2026-10-16 by sound effects I generated in ElevenLabs under my
commercial plan: every file of the table above except `gate_bell.wav`, `intro_bell.wav` and
`intro_wind.wav`, which stay computed. Each kept its name, so no code had to learn a new file.
The sources were 48 kHz stereo WAV files; each was made with ffmpeg alone:

1. Converted to 44 100 Hz in 32 bit float (soxr), and to mono where the old file was mono. The
   engine places no sound in space (every sound is as loud in both ears), so mono loses nothing;
   the crystal, the heartstone and the gate stay stereo as before.
2. Silence trimmed at both ends (`silenceremove`, below -55 dB RMS, 5 ms kept before the onset
   and 20 ms after the tail), a fade of 2 ms in and 20 ms out, so the natural tail stays.
3. One plain gain so the loudest 400 ms (momentary loudness, the file padded with a second of
   silence) equals that of the old file, so the mix keeps its balance. Two files were held a
   little lower so their true peak stays at -1.2 dBTP: `gate_open.wav` (0.4 dB under the old one)
   and `caught.wav` (2.2 dB under).
4. 16 bit with triangular dither.

The two loops: `maze_wind.wav` (10 s source) and `shade_near.wav` (4 s source). The last second
of the wind (the last half second of the hum) is crossfaded into its head (`acrossfade`,
`qsin`), which makes loops of 9 s and 3.5 s, and each file is turned round to start at a sample
near 0 with a small step after it. The step from the last sample to the first is 30 units of
16 bit for the wind (its ordinary step: median 31, 99 % 143) and 4 for the hum (median 23,
99 % 121); the loudness of each half second around the seam stays within the range of the
file. The wind is matched by integrated loudness (-43.4 LUFS, the old one -43.5) and the hum to -23.0
LUFS, near the loudest moment of the old hum (-22.8). The hum is a loop in the game now: on while the shade
is within 14 m of way, louder the nearer it is (0.8 at 0 m to 0.2 at 14 m,
`game::shadeHumVolume`), where the old one was a short swell repeated faster.

a package) and writes the same bytes every time.

in place of 12.8 MB. The script writes the format itself. The game decodes it once at its start

### The recordings

The four sources are 48 kHz stereo WAV files from ElevenLabs. Each one was turned into its
file with ffmpeg alone, in this order:

1. Converted to 44 100 Hz in 32 bit float (`aresample=44100:resampler=soxr:precision=28`),
   so every later cut is at a sample of the final rate.
2. A loop is cut as a body from `S` to `E` seconds of the source, and the `X` seconds after
   `E` are crossfaded into its head: `atrim` takes `[S+X, E+X)` and `[S, S+X)`, and
   `acrossfade` lays the end of the first over the second. The body begins at `S+X` and ends
   with the source just before `S+X`, so the loop goes on without a seam. The points:
   `menu_theme_v2` 0 to 108 s with 2 s (`tri`), on the 4 s bar the piece has, so the last bar
   leads into the first; `maze_ambient` 52 to 100 s with 4 s (`qsin`), three of its 16 s swells
   after its fade in and before its fade out; `shade_chase` 0 to 52 s with 4 s (`qsin`), on its
   1 s pulse and before its fade out. Each end was chosen by how well the loudness of the
   seconds after it matches the head (correlation of the 10 ms envelope).
3. The body is turned round (`atrim` and `concat`) so the file begins at a sample where both
   channels are near 0 and the loudness before and after is the same: the game converts the
   file to the rate of the device, and that conversion starts from silence.
4. One plain gain to the target (`volume`, measured on the body with `ebur128`): -2.9 dB for
   the theme, -8.3 dB for the bed, -12.9 dB for the chase, -3.0 dB for the sting. No
   compressor and no loudnorm. Then 16 bit with triangular dither once, and FLAC.

What the files measure (`ebur128=peak=true`, and the step from the last sample to the first
next to the steps inside the file, in 16 bit units):

| `menu_theme_v2.flac` | -24.0 LUFS | -6.7 dBTP | 95 / 99 | 58 / 424 |

## Updating

Run `python tools/make_sounds.py` from the repository root. It writes only the three sounds that
are still computed into `assets/audio` and leaves the recordings alone (`RECORDED` in the
script); with `--out-dir some/folder` it makes all twenty-three computed versions there, to
compare. The script writes the same bytes every time, so the files only change when the script
does. To change a sound, edit its function
in the script (each one lists its layers in a comment) and run the script again.

## Checking

Nobody can hear a table of numbers, but the numbers can show that a sound is built as intended:

- `python tools/make_sounds.py --out-dir some/folder` and then `--report --out-dir some/folder`
  (it refuses `assets/audio`, where most files are recordings now) reads the files back and prints their length, peak,
  loudness, DC offset, first and last sample, where their energy sits and their strongest
  frequencies, then checks them against the intent of every sound (for example: the pulse lies
  below 120 Hz, the crystal lies above 1 kHz and is inharmonic, the dead click has far less ring
  than the working one, the breath is shorter than the wait between two breaths, the flask
  lies below 1 kHz and is in tune, so it cannot be taken for the crystal, the hum of the shadow lies
  between the pulse and the breath and is shorter than the fastest wait between two hums, the
  steps are quieter than a crystal and than the hum, the steps of the shadow are quieter and
  duller than the ones of the player, the wind of the maze is quieter than all of them, the
  alert of the shadow is sudden, lies between 500 Hz and 1 kHz and is not in tune, the banish
  has no sudden start and nothing bright in it). For
  the loop it prints the numbers of its seam: the step and the bend from its last samples to its
  first ones, next to what is ordinary inside the file. It ends with the number of checks that
  missed.
- `tests/AudioEngineTests.cpp` plays the loop through the audio engine without a sound device
  and looks at the samples that would go to the loudspeaker: no step where the file starts
  again, and none while the loop fades in and out.
- `python tools/make_sounds.py --pictures some/folder` writes one PNG per file: the waveform
  above the spectrogram.

Whether a sound is pleasant, whether the pulse is quiet enough to repeat for a minute and
whether the breath sounds like one, is for a listener to decide, in the game, with headphones and with loudspeakers.

## Replacing a file by a recording

Keep the file name and the format (a WAV file, or FLAC for the theme: the build of miniaudio decodes nothing else)
and write its source, licence and author into the table above. In the script, take the sound
out of the list `SOUNDS`, so the next run does not write over the recording, and take its lines
out of `checks()`, which describe the generated sound and not the recording. A recording that
replaces the loop has to be a loop itself, and has to start where its wave crosses 0: the game
converts it to the sample rate of the sound device when it loads it, and that conversion starts
from silence, so any other start ticks once per round.
