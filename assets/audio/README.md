# Sounds

Own work, no third-party material. The program reads the files at start
(`src/game/NightMazeApp.cpp`, through `audio::AudioEngine::load`) and plays them when the rules
in `src/game/SoundCues.cpp` say so. That file also holds the table that gives every sound cue
its file name.

All twenty-three files are computed by `tools/make_sounds.py` from sine waves and noise. Nothing is
recorded and nothing is downloaded. The script uses only the standard library of Python (no
numpy, no other package). The files are 16 bit, 44 100 samples per second, mono except for the
crystal, the heartstone and the gate, which are stereo. The wind of the maze has half that sample rate: nothing
in it lies above 3 kHz, so the file is half the size and sounds the same. The twenty-one sounds
of a round are about 2.3 MB together (the wind of the maze is 0.44 MB of that, the five steps
0.13 MB, the alert and the banish of the shadow 0.12 MB, the bell of the gate 0.23 MB, the
heartstone 0.39 MB). The wind of the intro is as long as the intro itself (30 seconds) and is 2.6 MB alone.

The game mixes the sounds in two groups, each with a volume of its own on the settings screen:
the two winds are "ambient", everything else is "effects" (the table in
`src/game/SoundCues.cpp` says which).

The peaks differ on purpose: they are the mix of the game. The crystal and the gate stand in
front, the heartstone just behind them, the lever a step behind, the switch of the lamp is small, and the pulse and the breath
are the quietest because they repeat. No file peaks above -3 dBFS. The steps of the player are
heard twice a second for a whole round and stand below the hum of the shadow, the steps of the
shadow below those, and the wind of the maze below every sound that tells the player something.
The alert of the shadow stands a little above its hum, because it is heard once and must not be
missed. The banish is as soft as the hum: it is good news. The bell of the gate repeats until
the round ends, so it stands below the one bell of the intro.

## Updating

Run `python tools/make_sounds.py` from the repository root. The script writes the same bytes
every time, so the files only change when the script does. To change a sound, edit its function
in the script (each one lists its layers in a comment) and run the script again.

## Checking

Nobody can hear a table of numbers, but the numbers can show that a sound is built as intended:

- `python tools/make_sounds.py --report` reads the files back and prints their length, peak,
  loudness, DC offset, first and last sample, where their energy sits and their strongest
  frequencies, then checks them against the intent of every sound (for example: the pulse lies
  below 120 Hz, the crystal lies above 1 kHz and is inharmonic, the dead click has far less ring
  than the working one, the breath is shorter than the wait between two breaths, the flask
  lies below 1 kHz and is in tune, so it cannot be taken for the crystal, the hum of the shadow lies
  between the pulse and the breath and is shorter than the fastest wait between two hums, the
  steps are quieter than a crystal and than the hum, the steps of the shadow are quieter and
  duller than the ones of the player, the wind of the maze is quieter than all of them, the
  alert of the shadow is sudden, lies between 500 Hz and 1 kHz and is not in tune, the banish
  has no sudden start and nothing bright in it). For
  the loop it prints the numbers of its seam: the step and the bend from its last samples to its
  first ones, next to what is ordinary inside the file. It ends with the number of checks that
  missed.
- `tests/AudioEngineTests.cpp` plays the loop through the audio engine without a sound device
  and looks at the samples that would go to the loudspeaker: no step where the file starts
  again, and none while the loop fades in and out.
- `python tools/make_sounds.py --pictures some/folder` writes one PNG per file: the waveform
  above the spectrogram.

Whether a sound is pleasant, whether the pulse is quiet enough to repeat for a minute and
whether the breath sounds like one, is for a listener to decide, in the game, with headphones and with loudspeakers.

## Replacing a file by a recording

Keep the file name and the format (a WAV file: the build of miniaudio decodes nothing else)
and write its source, licence and author into the table above. In the script, take the sound
out of the list `SOUNDS`, so the next run does not write over the recording, and take its lines
out of `checks()`, which describe the generated sound and not the recording. A recording that
replaces the loop has to be a loop itself, and has to start where its wave crosses 0: the game
converts it to the sample rate of the sound device when it loads it, and that conversion starts
from silence, so any other start ticks once per round.
