# The land around the maze is real models in two bands inside 40 m of the border, and the ridge stays drawn with the sky
Status: accepted (2026-10-15), built 2026-10-28 with stand-in models, which got their own generated models on 2026-10-29 (below); it is the next release, before the tumbled walls and the gate seals
Code: src/game/Land.hpp, src/game/Land.cpp, src/game/LandMeshes.cpp, src/game/LandRenderer.cpp, src/game/MazeWorld.cpp, src/game/Minimap.cpp, src/game/NightMazeAppPasses.cpp, src/game/NightMazeAppRender.cpp, src/game/Shadows.cpp, src/debug/PerfProbe.cpp, tools/blender/build_land_pieces.py, tools/blender/build_land_hedge.py, tests/LandTests.cpp

Context: Beyond the border wall there is the margin of the terrain (`TERRAIN_MARGIN`, 14 m of hills up to 4.5 m, a sparse scatter of grass) and then nothing until the ridge, which is drawn with the sky about 79 m out on the screen ([village-on-the-ridge](village-on-the-ridge.md)). The end lines say the light walks across the land, and the land is empty. The planned landmarks over the walls were a few lone shapes on that empty land.

Decision, what the player sees: In the corridors, the tops of trees, roofs and the landmarks over the walls, and the land rising on three sides into a moonlit rim in front of the ridge. From 2 m in front of a 3 m wall a piece 5 m past it shows only above 6.2 m, from 4 m back above 4.6 m, so the near band reads by its trees and roofs, which stand on the 3 to 4.5 m hills of the margin. Field walls, the rick and the bridge are seen only from above: on the reveal over the exit, in the menu glide (14 to 22 m up) and in noclip. The reveal is the showcase: the camera looks across the valley on the village side, its lit windows in the lower half of the picture and the village above them.

Decision, the three bands, measured from the outer face of the border wall:
- Near band, 1.5 to 14 m: the existing terrain margin, so it stands in the moon shadow box and casts moon shadows from the still layer ([moon-shadow-still-layer](moon-shadow-still-layer.md)). Generated models at full detail: oaks, hawthorns, a hay rick, one or two farmhouses. Script-built: drystone field walls 1.2 m high wearing `wall_stone.png`, a round sheepfold of the same wall, hedgerows (a lumpy strip wearing a generated hedge picture). Each piece stands on `Terrain::lowestHeightUnder` and sinks 0.1 to 0.2 m, like the walls.
- Middle band, 14 to 40 m: a ring of ground of its own (`LandRing`), built in code like the terrain at a coarser 2 m spacing, its inner edge taking the heights of the terrain edge so no crack shows, and scaled by the same height scale. On the three sides away from the village it rises to a crest of 8 to 12 m at 30 to 36 m, then drops below y = 0 by 45 m, so its outer edge lies in fog for every eye the game uses (at 0 m and 84 m away the fog is 99.99 %). On the village side it falls into a valley with a stream at y 0 to 0.3 m. On it: copses (one generated clump of bare trees, turned and scaled), cottages with windows, the landmarks on the crests. Pieces here are the same rolls decimated by the build to a far version (about 1,500 triangles a tree, 3,000 a landmark): no transparency, so no flat cards.
- Far band: the ridge and the village as today, eye-centred with real depth. Depth comes from the real middle band moving in front of it. Two more eye-centred silhouettes stand on the ridge on other bearings, a mill and a church tower, each with its own model of light, built around the eye like the village (nothing past `VILLAGE_REACH`).

Decision, the far plane stays 100 m: real geometry ends 40 m past the border, so from the far side of the largest campaign maze (44 m) the furthest piece is 84 m away, inside the far plane and in front of most of the ridge (79 to 88 m on the screen). A crest 10 m high 84 m away is about 5 % fogged and stands above the ridge line (1 degree up), which is what gives the rim its depth. Mazes of the debug window beyond 22 cells may lose the far edge of the land; that is accepted.

Decision, placement: a generator of its own (`std::mt19937` and the range helper of [deterministic-random](deterministic-random.md)), seeded from the maze seed and a constant of the land, drawn after everything in the maze, so no existing maze, Tonight's hedge, test seed or the menu seed changes. Every piece keeps its box 1.5 m clear of the outer face of the border wall, out of the grass strip, and inside 40 m. Field walls and hedgerows follow a seeded field grid of 12 to 20 m fields turned a few degrees, and no piece stands on a field line. On the village side a candidate is rejected when its top rises above 5 degrees from the reveal camera (5 m over the exit cell, tested with the real exit of that maze) within 8 degrees either side of the bearing to the village: at 20 m that is 6.8 m, at 40 m 8.5 m. Nothing tall stands on the village side but the farmhouse in the valley below that line.

Decision, the lit windows: the build of each house places window quads by rays, like the village, as slots in the model's space. At load the game collects the slots of every placed building in the land and deals them to the nights in a seeded order: 20, 25, 25 and 30 % to nights 1 to 4, and none to night 5, whose one new light stays the player's window. The mill lights with night 2 and the church tower with night 4. How many nights are lit is `villageNightsLit` and nothing else, so free play, Tonight's hedge and the menus show the progress of the campaign, the intro and a tool run without `--night` show none, and a night shows the nights before it. The new share fades in with `villageNewLightStrength` during the reveal. A glow is one value per draw, so the land lights are one mesh per night, built at load: at most 4 draws, plus one each for the mill and the church. By night 5 the valley is lit.

Decision, the landmarks: the five outer landmarks of the old plan (standing stone, dovecote, chapel gable, watch hut, cairn) become the tallest pieces of the middle band. A maze gets three, one on each crest away from the village (the village is the landmark of its own side), chosen by the seed, 25 to 35 m out, so they stand 5 to 8 m above the crest and read over the walls (a 7 m top 35 m away is 25 % fogged). The hawthorn is no longer inside the maze: it is a near band tree. A landmark counts as seen once a ray from the eye to its top clears every wall it crosses while the top is in the view frustum. The held map then shows its icon on the edge of the map at its bearing, the way the tick of the open gate sits on the edge.

Decision, fog and light: the land uses the fog as it is; no mist cards (the engine draws no transparency). The valley floor lies below the fog base (0.5 m), so the mist pools in it by itself. The ring and the middle band are lit by the moon and the ambient light of the lit program, and the ring lies outside the moon shadow box, where the sampler returns its border and everything is lit. The flashlight (10 m) does not reach past the border, and its pass culls the land away. Windows are amber glow pieces wearing `lantern.png`, left to the bloom, which is taken before the fog ([bloom-input](bloom-input.md)), so a window in the mist of the valley still glows. No window casts light. The land is left out of the reflection pass.

Decision, what stays scripted: the ring of ground, the field walls, the sheepfold, the hedgerows, the stream (a dark strip along the valley), the window quads, the placement and the dealing of the lights, the map icons, and the sails of the mill if Tripo draws them as lattice.

Why: I rejected widening the terrain margin to 40 m: the moon shadow box is fitted to the terrain, so its texels would about double (48 m of land across a default maze would become 100 m). I rejected raising the far plane so the land could reach the ridge: the village, its fog test and `VILLAGE_CLEAR_EYE_HEIGHT` are tuned to 100 m, and past 40 m the ground is lost in the fog anyway (bare ground is 93 % fogged at 30 m). I rejected low field pieces in the middle band for the same reason: there only what is high reads, so the money goes into trees, roofs and landmarks.

Cost and revisit, the budget: before culling the land adds at most 90 draw calls (about 30 near band models, 40 far versions, 3 landmarks, 1 ring, 1 mesh of field walls and the fold, 1 of hedgerows, up to 6 of lights) and at most 150,000 triangles, against 592,787 a frame today on the 4070 Ti. After culling a corridor view should add 20 to 40 draws. Measure it like the culling record (Release, 1280 x 720, `--play --calm --seed 7`, the 4070 Ti and the M3, standing at the start and on the reveal). I reopen it if the M3 frame in play grows by more than 1 ms, and then merge the far versions of each side into one mesh. The reveal is not a trigger: its cost was accepted (below). The menu video, the launcher loop and the README pictures are recorded again.

Decided by the user on 2026-10-15: night 5 lights no land windows, so the player's window is its one new light; three landmarks a maze, one per crest; landmark icons on the edge of the map at the bearing; the far band gets the mill and the church tower.

Still open, with the default the plan assumes:
1. The hawthorn: only outer trees (default), or one more inside the maze in a free corner cell with the trunk box of the old plan, which joins the exclusion rules of the sheep.
2. The stream: a dark matte strip (default), or the reflection of the puddles laid along it.

Built on 2026-10-28, what differs from the plan:
- Stand-ins, built 2026-10-28 until their own model was generated: the oak was the rowan, the hay rick the peat stack, the copse three dead oaks, the standing stone the granite tor, the dovecote and the cottage the moor cottage, the watch hut the log woodshed, the mill and the farmhouse the stone cottage, the barn the ruined barn and the church tower the chapel ruin. The bridge is not built.
- Their own models, swapped in on 2026-10-29, each at the height of its old line: the oak a bare ancient oak (10 m, radius 3.9 m: its roots and crown are wider than the rowan; it stays in the near band only), the rick a thatched rick with a ladder (4 m, 2,500 triangles, up from 1,500, so the ladder keeps its rungs), the copse one model of a clump on its own mound (`copies=1`, 9 m built, 8.6 m after decimation), the standing stone a stone with a hole (5.5 m), the dovecote a round dovecote (6.5 m built, 6.3 m: the decimation takes its finial), the watch hut a hut on a stone base (5 m), the farmhouse a stone farmhouse (7 m, eight windows), the barn an L-shaped farmstead (6.5 m, radius 5.6 m), the cottage a cottage with a yard wall (5.5 m built, 5.4 m, four windows), the mill a windmill (7 m) and the church tower a square tower with a traceried arch (7 m). The windmill has its sails on its back, so its line turns it half round (`turn`) and the eye on the ridge sees the sails. The rays find no window on the mill or the tower, so each line sets where its one light stands (`fallback_height`): the mill below its sails, the tower in its arch, whose lancets are too narrow for the rays. The chapel gable, the cairn, the hawthorn, the dead oak and the woodshed were already their own models.
- The ridge church (a church with its tower and nave) was generated too and is not used. It would replace the church tower: as a far silhouette it reads as a church, but the rays find 40 windows on it, many of them dark stones, and its lights stand scattered over the nave. Beside the tower it would put two towers on one bearing, next to the tower of the village, and a third silhouette is a new place on the ridge, a new night of light and two more draws. The lone tower reads clearly at that distance with its one lit arch.
- The pictures stay at the sizes of the old lines (1,024 for the houses and landmarks, 512 for the trees, the rick and the watch hut), but a piece no longer shares the picture of another, so the land wears 16 pictures where it wore 11: about 10 million texels a map where it had 7.25 million (colour and normal map each).
- Swapping in a generated model: copy its GLB to `tools/blender/sources/<name>_tripo.glb`, point its line of `PIECES` at it, run the script, and copy the height and half width it prints into `MODEL_SHAPES` in `Land.cpp` (the radius is about 0.7 of the half width for a tree, about all of it for a building). `tests/LandTests.cpp` checks both against the files, and the budget and the views over the walls.
- The near band was made taller on 2026-10-28, so that a crown or a roof shows over the walls from most corridors that run to the border: the oak 10 m, the hawthorn 6 m, the dead oak 9 m, the farmhouse 7 m, the barn 6.5 m, scaled 0.85 to 1.15, and up to 17 near trees, one every 10 m of the band. Of the level views down such a corridor (eye of the player, field of view of the settings, 18 mazes) 65 % show a near crown or roof and 81 % something of the land, against 41 and 65 % before (a test keeps them over 60 and 75 %).
- The land and the ridge get a little more light than the moon gives them, because the reveal showed the ridge as a flat black cut-out: the lit program adds a sky fill (more on faces that look up) and, on the pieces only, a rim on faces seen edge-on, both growing over the 14 m of the margin from nothing at the border wall, so the maze is not lit differently. The textured program adds a little moon and sky light to the village and the far silhouettes. Both stay below the brightness of the sky. The Gouraud mode does not have it.
- No grass grows through the field walls, the hedgerows and the buildings (`landCoversGround`). Before, every maze had some, 96 tufts on average.
- The ring wears `ground.png` like the terrain, not a meadow picture of its own. The hedge picture is a side view of a generated hedge (`build_land_hedge.py`).
- The land windows glow at 8, more than the village, because they stand in the thick of the fog and only the bloom carries them.
- The perf probe takes `land` and `noland` to switch the land, `fov=` and `map`, and `shot=` writes the frame to a file.

Measured on the M3 (2026-10-28, Release, `--play --calm --seed 7`, the window size of the settings), after the taller near band: at the start the land adds 35 draws and 91,000 triangles after culling, and 79 draws and 215,000 triangles with culling off (both shadow passes included). Before it, 27 draws and 69,000 triangles, and 76 and 197,000. On the reveal (fov 22) it adds 16 draws and 31,000 triangles. The pieces of one maze are at most 82 models and 140,000 triangles, and the meshes built in code at most 12,000 (`tests/LandTests.cpp`). At the start the frame time does not move (10.1 ms either way). On the reveal it grows by about 1.15 ms (5.6 to 6.8 ms), all of it in the scene pass. It is fill, not draws: the land now covers half of that picture, where the sky was, with the lit program. Leaving out the ring saves about 0.35 ms of it and leaving out the pieces about 0.3 ms, so merging the far versions would not win it back. Accepted by the user on 2026-10-28: the reveal lasts a few seconds, so the 1.15 ms on the M3 stays and does not reopen this record. The taller and denser near band, measured after that, raised it to about 1.6 ms (5.6 to 7.2 ms); the added light costs nothing measurable. The 4070 Ti is not measured.
Measured again after the swap (2026-10-29, the M3, the same command, the reveal standing 5 m over the exit cell at fov 22, the old and the new build taken in turn, three rounds of 5 s each, started only when no other process was busy): the land adds 2.0 ms to the median frame before the swap (5.9 to 7.9 ms) and 1.9 ms after it (5.7 to 7.6 ms); the scene pass 1.5 and 1.2 ms. A second pair, measured while another game ran, gave 2.4 and 1.3 ms. On the reveal the frame has 140 draws and 308,000 triangles, where it had 143 and 307,000. The swap costs nothing; if anything the new models are a little cheaper. Both builds measure about 0.3 ms over the 1.6 ms of 2026-10-28, which is the machine of that day, not the land.
