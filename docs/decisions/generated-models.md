# Generated models replace the script-built ones, and each is a committed source with a deterministic script
Status: accepted (2026-10-15)
Code: tools/blender/sources, tools/blender/generated_common.py, tools/blender/build_lamp_held.py, tools/blender/build_splinter.py, tools/blender/build_stone_sheep.py, tools/blender/build_stile.py, tools/blender/build_flask.py, tools/blender/build_milestone.py, tools/blender/build_village.py, tools/blender/build_gate.py, tools/blender/build_gate_arch.py, tools/blender/build_gate_lantern.py, tools/blender/build_gate_bell.py, tools/blender/bake_wall_normals.py, tools/blender/make_textures.py, src/game/Lighting.hpp, src/game/Crystals.hpp, src/game/StoneSheep.hpp, src/game/GateLamp.hpp, src/game/GateLamp.cpp, src/game/VillageRenderer.cpp

Context: the shade was the first generated model ([generated-shade-model](generated-shade-model.md)), and the rest of the game still looked built from boxes and rings. I generated the lamp, the splinters, the heartstone, the sheep, the stile post, the flask, the milestone, the village, the door, the gatehouse, its lantern and its bell, and three wall pictures in Tripo.
Decision: every one is a committed source (`<name>_tripo.glb` or a picture under `tools/blender/sources/`) and its `build_<name>.py` writes the game files with the same bytes on every run, using the helpers of `generated_common.py`. Surfaces are smooth and keep the UVs of the source. Glow pieces stay script-built and flat: the lens and window of the lamp, the window lights of the village, the ridge ring, the hooks of the arch, the panes of the lantern. Departures from the source: the sheep is 0.75 m long (was 0.9) and scaled across to 0.4 m, the milestone is 0.75 m tall, and the arch has a 1.5 m doorway, no cote, the big lantern on the crown and a slab closing the half-moon over the door. The wall normal maps of stone and mossy are baked from their slabs and warped onto the picture by a smooth shift field (stone median 0 px, max 2.2 px; mossy median 1 px, one patch at 21 px over a hole of the slab); cracked has no slab, so it is derived from the picture. The bake takes about 50 s, so `bake_wall_normals.py` writes committed intermediates and `make_textures.py` only reads PNGs. Walls are darker, `WALL_LIGHT` 0.24 (was 0.294), because at 0.29 about 1 % of the pixels clipped to white. Follow-up of 2026-10-15: the source of the plain wall was two copies of one picture, so `make_textures.py` keeps one and mirrors it into the other half; every wall picture gets level joints at v = 0 and 0.3, and the wall models put v = 0 at the top of the plinth, so the plinth and the coping meet joints. The door is drawn with a highlight of 0.04 (`GATE_DOOR_SPECULAR_STRENGTH`): with the 0.25 of the stone the flashlight laid one sheen over wood and iron and the door read as a melted shape. Its iron bands are painted black (the source painted them like the wood on one face), its grain is pushed out and its normal map made 2.5 times steeper. The arch keeps its pictures at 2048 (at 1024 its stones had about 130 texels a metre against the 256 of the walls), its normal map 1.5 times steeper, and the slab over the door is laid on a patch of dressed stones of the picture instead of one flat colour. The window of the lamp is a painted frame with three panes.
Why: I rejected committing only the outputs, for the reason of the shade: an output as the source cannot be redone with another size or colour. I rejected widening the box of the sheep to fit the 0.65 m of the lying model: at 0.66 m the tests broke the rules they keep (a way past of 1.2 m with 0.4 m each side of the path of the shade, every cell reachable), and even 0.5 m failed. Scaling gives a reach of 0.341 m on a diagonal, inside the 0.35 m of the place. A 2 m doorway left each pier 1.3 m wide and 2.5 m deep, stone where the player walks, and at 1 m the milestone is 0.37 m thick.
Cost and revisit: the batch is about 102,000 triangles in all (the arch alone 28,463, the village 9,028, the door 6,684). Measured on an RTX 4070 Ti (2026-10-15, Release, window of 1280 x 720, seed 6, the first corridor of Hard): 592,787 triangles a frame against 173,511 before, the frame 1.01 against 0.98 ms, one reading of the Diagnostics category each. Still off: the niches of `wall_damaged` read as dark patches, the door keeps the lumpy shape of its source, the heartstone keeps a few small holes of its source, and a few models have mirrored triangles (up to 102 on the milestone), which make the loader warn. The rows of a wall picture up to 0.3 are seen twice on a 3 m wall. I reopen it when the frame time of a slower card says the triangles matter.
