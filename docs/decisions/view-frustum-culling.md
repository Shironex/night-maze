# A pass does not draw what lies outside its own frustum
Status: accepted (2026-10-13)
Code: src/scene/Frustum.hpp, src/scene/Frustum.cpp, src/assets/ObjLoader.hpp, src/assets/AssetCache.hpp, src/game/ModelDraw.cpp, src/game/MazeRenderer.hpp, src/game/MazeRenderer.cpp, src/game/GameplayRenderer.cpp, src/game/InteractableRenderer.cpp, src/game/NightMazeAppPasses.cpp, src/game/NightMazeAppRound.cpp, src/gfx/RenderStats.hpp, src/debug/categories/DiagnosticsCategory.cpp

Context: Every wall and every pillar is a draw call of its own, and every pass handed all of them to the card: 1,353 draw calls in a frame on Normal and 2,360 on Hard, of which the flashlight pass alone made 638 and 1,134 for a cone that reaches 10 m.
Decision: `scene::frustumFromMatrix` reads the six planes of a frustum out of a view-projection matrix (the method of Gribb and Hartmann, which works for a perspective and for an orthographic matrix), and `scene::canBeSeen` rejects a box when the corner that reaches farthest along the normal of one plane is still behind that plane. The scene pass and the reflection pass test against the planes of the matrices they are given, the flashlight pass against the planes of its own light space. The boxes hold what is drawn, not what collides: `assets::vertexBounds` computes the bounds of a model from its vertices at load, `MazeRenderer::bounds` moves them into the world once per maze (`game::MazeBounds`, built by `uploadGround`), and `game::drawModel` does the same in every call for the things that move. The box of a wall is the union of its plain model and the model of its look, because the wall variants can be switched off in any frame. The box of a wall that a lever lowers reaches down by `GATE_SINK_DEPTH`, its whole way. A switch in the Diagnostics category turns the test off.
Why: The planes of the camera for the shadow pass were rejected: a pillar behind the player is outside the picture, and its shadow is not. The collision boxes as bounds were rejected: a crowned wall is higher than its collision box and would vanish while its crown is still in view. A box per lever wall that follows the wall down was rejected for the one that covers the travel: it needs no update, and it only keeps a sunk wall in the list while the place it stood on is in view. The test is conservative on purpose: a box near a corner of the frustum can be kept although it is outside, and a box is only left out when it is more than 1 cm behind a plane (`FRUSTUM_MARGIN`), so a rounding error never removes something visible. The moon pass does not cull, because its box holds the whole land. The terrain, the grass, the puddles, the village, the sky and the held lamp are one or a few draw calls each and are always drawn. I checked the picture with a temporary probe that drew the same frame twice, with and without the test, and compared the scene colour, the scene depth and the depth of the flashlight map texel for texel (Release, Apple M3, seed 7, a framebuffer of 2560 x 1440): a scripted camera that flies through cells and walls, turns, looks up and down and along the corridors while the lever walls sink (3,000 frames on Normal, 3,000 on Hard, 1,500 on Hard with the gate sinking, 1,500 on seed 3), and the two shots of the menu camera on both difficulties (1,500 frames each). The depth of the scene and the flashlight map differed in 0 texels in every frame. The colour differed in 0 texels once the probe drew the moon map once for both pictures and left the grass out, except for 2 texels in one frame of one run that a second run of the same script did not show. Before that a few frames differed by some thousand texels of colour, and so did two pictures drawn with the same setting: drawing the moon map or the grass twice in one frame is not exactly repeatable on this card, with or without the test.
Cost and revisit: Six dot products per box and plane: all 582 boxes of a maze on Normal take 3 to 8 microseconds per pass. Measured on an Apple M3 (2026-10-13, Release, vsync off, window of 1280 x 720, `--play --calm --seed 7`, three runs of 14 s with the switch on and three with it off in turn, the samples after the first 4 s, median): standing at the start, a frame went from 1,353 to 449 draw calls on Normal (flashlight 638 to 29, scene 611 to 327, reflections 33 to 22) and from 2,360 to 713 on Hard (flashlight 1,134 to 27, scene 1,093 to 577). From a point 22 m above the maze it went to 702 and 1,225: nearly all of the scene is in view there, and only the flashlight pass is saved. The CPU render time went from 5.07 to 3.79 ms on Normal and from 5.80 to 4.91 ms on Hard, and the flashlight pass on the card from 0.26 to 0.03 ms and from 0.45 to 0.01 ms. The scene pass on the card did not move (5.32 and 4.92 ms on Normal, 5.80 and 6.08 ms on Hard, the samples of one setting lie 4 to 7 ms apart). The frame time went from 12.41 to 11.47 ms on Normal and from 14.29 to 13.27 ms on Hard, which is no more than the samples of one setting spread (about 5 ms on Normal and 13 ms on Hard): the frame still waits for the card. With the switch off the draw calls are those of the commit before. I reopen this if the walls are drawn instanced (the test then fills an instance buffer), or if walls hidden behind other walls are to be left out too, which planes cannot do.
