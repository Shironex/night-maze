# The exit is the cell farthest from the start by passages, and a lit gatehouse marks it
Status: accepted (2026-10-08)
Code: src/game/Exit.hpp, src/game/Exit.cpp, src/game/MazeWorld.cpp, src/game/GateLamp.hpp, src/game/GateLamp.cpp, src/game/SoundCues.hpp, tests/ExitTests.cpp, tests/GateLampTests.cpp

Context: One gate segment must close the exit, the walk should be long, and a seed must give the same exit everywhere. After playing, the gate looked like any other corner, and players took corners for the exit.
Decision: A breadth-first search from the start counts passages. The cell with the largest count is the exit, ties going to the first in row order. The gate stands on its only open side, under a stone gatehouse 6 m high with three lanterns: a big one 5.1 m up, above the 3 m walls, and two at the door. The lanterns are never off. Closed, they hold a cold ember in the colour of the moon that grows with every crystal (0.2 to 0.4 of the full glow). When the gate opens they turn amber over the 1.5 s the door sinks, with one amber point light of 6 m at the door, a bell every 6 s that is louder the fewer passages lie between me and the exit, and a tick on the edge of the map. The last two cells grow no grass and a milestone stands in the one before the gate.
Why: In a perfect maze the farthest cell is always a dead end, so one gate closes it. The opposite corner can have two open sides, and in the 4 by 4 test maze it is 6 passages away while the farthest cell is 14. A lantern above the walls is the only mark that shows from another corridor, a lamp that is dark until the gate opens would not help the search before that, and amber is the one warm colour of the night.
Cost and revisit: The exit can be near in a straight line. The lantern only shows down a corridor that points at it, the bell says nearer or farther and never left or right, and the warm light passes through walls. I reopen it if mazes gain loops or the start moves, and I retune the lamp and the bell by playing. Earlier: a plain wooden gate between two pillars, with no light, no sound and no mark on the map before its cell was seen.
