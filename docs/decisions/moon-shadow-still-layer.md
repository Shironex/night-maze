# The moon shadow map keeps the casters that never move and redraws only the others
Status: accepted (2026-10-12)
Code: src/game/ShadowMap.hpp, src/game/ShadowMap.cpp, src/game/Shadows.hpp, src/game/MazeWorld.hpp, src/game/MazeRenderer.cpp, src/game/NightMazeAppPasses.cpp, src/game/NightMazeAppRound.cpp, src/gfx/Framebuffer.cpp, src/gfx/Shader.hpp

Context: The moon pass drew every caster in every frame: 638 of the 1,935 draw calls of a frame on Normal and 1,134 of 3,423 on Hard. The box of the moon does not follow the camera, so most of that picture is the same from frame to frame. A plain "redraw when dirty" flag does not help: crystals and flasks bob in every frame.
Decision: `ShadowMap` has a second depth texture of the same size, the still layer. It holds the terrain, the walls no lever lowers, the pillars, the post of the stile and the stone sheep (`MazeParts::Fixed`). Each frame the layer is copied into the map with `glBlitFramebuffer` (`Framebuffer::copyDepthTo`) in place of the clear, and the other casters are drawn on top with the depth test. The layer is drawn again when its `StaticShadowKey` is not the one of the frame: the view and the projection of the light, the size of the map, a number `uploadGround` counts up for every new maze and every new terrain, the wall variants switch, and `Shader::loadCount` of the depth program. A wall that a lever lowers is drawn with the moving casters for the whole round, pulled or not. So is everything `GameplayRenderer` and `InteractableRenderer` draw, the gatehouse included.
Why: Marking the layer dirty from every place that changes one of these things was rejected: a place that forgets leaves the shadow of a wall that is gone, and the key costs one comparison of two matrices and four numbers a frame. Moving a lever wall into the layer while it stands was rejected too: it saves at most 16 draw calls and needs a redraw when the wall starts to sink and another when it lands. Updating the map every second frame was rejected because moving shadows would stutter. The picture is the same as before, because the nearest depth at a texel does not depend on the order the casters are drawn in. I checked that with a temporary read back of the map (Debug, Apple M3, seed 7, one run on Normal and three on Hard, one frame in every 20): with crystals bobbing, two walls sinking, and after each change of the key, every checked frame differed from a full redraw of the same frame in 0 texels.
Cost and revisit: One more depth texture of the size of the map, 12 to 16 MB at 2048 texels. Measured on an Apple M3 (2026-10-12, Release, vsync off, window of 1280 x 720, `--play --calm --seed 7`, three runs of each build in turn, the last three samples of each run): the moon pass went from 638 to 56 draw calls on Normal and from 1,134 to 71 on Hard, the frame from 1,935 to 1,353 and from 3,423 to 2,360. The median CPU render time went from 4.95 to 4.51 ms on Normal and from 5.92 to 5.68 ms on Hard, but the samples of one build spread over 1.2 ms, and the frame time did not move outside its own spread: the gain in time is inside the noise on this machine, which waits for the card and not for the draw calls. The timer query of the moon pass reports about 0 ms since the copy, so the time of this pass can no longer be read from it on this card. I reopen this if a caster of the layer starts to move (it then belongs to the other half), or if the map of the flashlight needs the same: its pyramid moves with the hand, so it has no still layer.
