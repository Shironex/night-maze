# The lamp in the hand is drawn on the axis of its beam, in front of every wall and in no shadow map
Status: accepted (2026-10-12)
Code: src/game/Lighting.hpp, src/game/Lighting.cpp, src/game/GameState.cpp, src/game/NightMazeAppPasses.cpp, src/game/GameplayRenderer.cpp, tools/blender/build_lamp_held.py, tests/LightingTests.cpp, tests/GameStateTests.cpp

Context: the flashlight is the one tool of the game and nothing of it was seen. Its light stands in the hand, 0.20 m right of the eye and 0.25 m below it, and no picture shows that place: at 60 degrees the picture reaches that far down only 0.43 m ahead.
Decision: the light stays where it is and the lamp is drawn 0.35 m further along its own beam, with the lens looking down the axis of the cone (`heldLampMatrix`), so the spot of light lies where the lamp points. Its hand hangs below the eye along the up vector of the camera, not straight down in the world like the hand of the light, so it keeps its place in the lower right at every pitch and still aims at the point the beam aims at. It is drawn last in the scene with the program of the lighting mode and a field of view of its own (60 degrees, `heldLampProjection`). Its depths are squeezed into the nearest hundredth of the depth range, so it is in front of every wall. The depth buffer is not cleared for it, because the fog of the composite pass reads the depths of the scene: the squeezed range puts the lamp at 0.1 m for the fog, which is none. The flashlight is taken out of the lights for that one draw, and the lamp is in neither shadow map: it lies in its own beam and would light its own back and darken the cone. It is shown only where the picture is taken from the eyes of the player (`showsHeldLamp`). The lens and the window in the body are models of their own, because a glow is one value per draw: the lens follows the beam and the window the battery.
Why: I rejected the hand of the light for the lamp too, which was built first: the lamp drifted into the middle of the picture of a player who looks at the ground, and hung there without an arm.
Cost and revisit: the lens faces away from the player, so only the beam and the click tell that the lamp is on, and in the dark the tin is nearly black. Looking straight up or down, the lamp is up to 0.35 m away from the light it stands for. It does not sway with the steps. There is no hand. I reopen it with an arm, or if the dark lamp reads badly in play.
