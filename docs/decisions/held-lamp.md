# The lamp in the hand is drawn on the axis of its beam, in front of every wall and in no shadow map
Status: accepted (2026-10-12)
Code: src/game/Lighting.hpp, src/game/Lighting.cpp, src/game/GameState.cpp, src/game/NightMazeAppPasses.cpp, src/game/GameplayRenderer.cpp, tools/blender/build_lamp_held.py, tests/LightingTests.cpp, tests/GameStateTests.cpp

Context: the flashlight is the one tool of the game and nothing of it was seen. Its light stands in the hand, 0.20 m right of the eye and 0.25 m below it, and no picture shows that place: at 60 degrees the picture reaches that far down only 0.43 m ahead.
Decision: the light stays where it is and the lamp is drawn 0.35 m further along its own beam, with the lens looking down the axis of the cone (`heldLampMatrix`), so the spot of light lies where the lamp points. Its hand hangs below the eye along the up vector of the camera, not straight down in the world like the hand of the light, so it keeps its place in the lower right at every pitch and still aims at the point the beam aims at. It is drawn last in the scene with the program of the lighting mode, a field of view of its own (60 degrees, `heldLampProjection`) and its depths squeezed into the nearest hundredth of the depth range, so it is in front of every wall. The flashlight is taken out of the lights for that one draw. It is in neither shadow map. It is shown only where the picture is taken from the eyes of the player (`showsHeldLamp`). The lens and the window in the body are models of their own, because a glow is one value per draw: the lens follows the beam and the window the battery.
Why: I rejected a lamp at the hand of the light with a wide lens of its own, which stretched it along the lower edge. Clearing the depth buffer was rejected because the fog of the composite pass reads the depths of the scene: the squeezed range puts the lamp at 0.1 m for the fog, which is none. With the hand of the light the lamp drifted into the middle of the picture of a player who looks at the ground, and hung there without an arm. In a shadow map of the flashlight the lamp would darken its own beam, and one rule for both maps leaves it out of the map of the moon too, where it would be a speck of one texel beside a player who casts no shadow.
Cost and revisit: the lens faces away from the player, so only the beam and the click tell that the lamp is on, and in the dark the tin is nearly black. Looking up or down, the lamp is up to 0.25 m away from the light it stands for. It does not sway with the steps. There is no hand. I reopen it with an arm, or if the dark lamp reads badly in play.
