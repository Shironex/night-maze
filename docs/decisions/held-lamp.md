# The lamp in the hand is drawn on the axis of its beam, in front of every wall and in no shadow map
Status: accepted (2026-10-15)
Code: src/game/Lighting.hpp, src/game/Lighting.cpp, src/game/GameState.cpp, src/game/NightMazeAppPasses.cpp, src/game/GameplayRenderer.cpp, tools/blender/build_lamp_held.py, tests/LightingTests.cpp, tests/GameStateTests.cpp

Context: the flashlight is the one tool of the game and nothing of it was seen. Its light stands in the hand, 0.20 m right of the eye and 0.25 m below it, and no picture shows that place: at 60 degrees the picture reaches that far down only 0.43 m ahead.
Decision: the light stays where it is and the lamp is drawn 0.35 m further along its own beam, with the lens looking down the axis of the cone (`heldLampMatrix`), so the spot of light lies where the lamp points. Its hand hangs below the eye along the up vector of the camera, not straight down in the world like the hand of the light, so it keeps its place in the lower right at every pitch and still aims at the point the beam aims at. It is drawn last in the scene with the program of the lighting mode and a field of view of its own (60 degrees, `heldLampProjection`). Its depths are squeezed into the nearest hundredth of the depth range, so it is in front of every wall. The depth buffer is not cleared for it, because the fog of the composite pass reads the depths of the scene: the squeezed range puts the lamp at 0.1 m for the fog, which is none. The flashlight is taken out of the lights for that one draw, and the lamp is in neither shadow map: it lies in its own beam and would light its own back and darken the cone. It is shown only where the picture is taken from the eyes of the player (`showsHeldLamp`). The model is a generated railway hand lamp, 0.17 m tall with its bail, upright with the lens in its side and turned so the lens looks down the beam (see [generated-models](generated-models.md)); its lens stands 0.113 m out (`HELD_LAMP_LENS_OUT`, was 0.219) and the window is a small grid on the back of the shoulder, facing the eye. The grid wears a painted patch of its own, a frame of dark tin around three panes that glow most in the middle and least under the brim of the cap: one flat colour over the pane read as a sticker on the tin (2026-10-15). The lens and the window in the body are models of their own, because a glow is one value per draw: the lens follows the beam and the window the battery. While the lamp is on, one weak point light is added to the lights of that one draw (`heldLampLights`): it stands at (-0.025, 0.05, -0.115) in the model, on the side of the player, has the colour of the beam and 0.6 of its intensity, flicker included, and reaches 0.1 m, so it touches the body and is gone at the window. The lamp sways with the steps (`advanceHeldLampSway`, `heldLampSwayPose`): 8 mm to each side and 5 mm down at each footfall in a walk, 14 mm and 10 mm in a sprint, with a roll of 1 and 2 degrees. The swing counts the metres walked with the stride of the step sounds, and comes in and settles over 0.25 s. The lamp falls behind a turning camera by a fifth of the turn, at most 2 degrees, and catches up in about 0.1 s; a turn of more than 45 degrees in one frame is the start of a round and leaves no lag. The sway is advanced with the frame time in `drawHeldLamp` while a round is played, and not under the pause menu, on the result screen or during a catch. Only the model moves: the light, its shadow map and `flashlightPose` are untouched.
Why: I rejected the hand of the light for the lamp too, which was built first: the lamp drifted into the middle of the picture of a player who looks at the ground, and hung there without an arm. For the light on the head I rejected a glow of the tin (`uEmissive`): it is one value per draw, so a part of the head would shine evenly like a thing that glows, and it would need a fourth model. A light fades with distance and angle for free. It cannot stand in the lens, because every face of the head the player sees looks away from the lens. The bob goes down and not up, and the lamp turns about its butt, because the butt lies just under the lower edge of the picture and nothing holds it. The swing counts metres and not seconds, so it does not jump when a walk becomes a sprint.
Cost and revisit: the light on the head is a fill light placed by hand, not light the lens gives off, and the unlit program does not show it. Switched off, the tin is nearly black in the dark. Looking straight up or down, the lamp is up to 0.35 m away from the light it stands for, and its lens is up to 1.4 cm beside the beam in the middle of a sprint, about 2 cm with a fast turn on top. A player who stands has a lamp that stands. The numbers of the sway are constants in `Lighting.hpp`, with no control in the debug window. There is no hand. I reopen it with an arm, or if the dark lamp reads badly in play.
