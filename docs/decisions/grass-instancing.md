# Grass is drawn by instancing by default, in chunks, and the geometry shader stays as a second path
Status: accepted (2026-10-16)
Code: assets/shaders/common/grass_blade.glsl, assets/shaders/grass_instanced.vert, assets/shaders/grass.vert, assets/shaders/grass.geom, src/game/Grass.cpp, src/game/GrassRenderer.cpp, src/game/NightMazeAppPasses.cpp, src/gfx/VertexArray.cpp, src/debug/categories/WorldCategory.cpp

Context: The grass was one draw call of points that `grass.geom` turned into three blades each, every tuft every frame, also behind the camera. On macOS the geometry stage is emulated by the driver, and the geometry shader is the demo of course topic 9, so it has to stay.
Decision: Both paths read one buffer of tufts, ordered by chunk (a 4 m grid, `groupGrassIntoChunks`) in the renderer only, so `placeGrass` and its tests are unchanged. The default path draws a mesh of one tuft (15 corners, 3 blades) with `glDrawElementsInstanced`, the tufts are an attribute with a divisor of 1. The blade math lives once in `common/grass_blade.glsl` and both programs include it. Chunks outside the frustum are left out in both paths, and the chunks that are drawn are merged into as few ranges as their order allows. OpenGL 4.1 has no base instance, so the two tuft attributes are pointed at the first tuft of a range before each call. With the distance LOD a tuft loses its third and then its second blade, shrunk away smoothly over 4 m with a random band per tuft, and the instanced path draws only the blades a far chunk still has. The World category switches the path, the culling, the LOD and its two distances.
Why: Instancing makes the same triangles without a geometry stage and keeps them countable (`countDraw` counts triangles times instances). Writing the blades into a vertex buffer on the CPU was rejected: three times the data, sent again for every wind frame or moved to a vertex shader anyway. Splitting the tufts into one buffer per chunk was rejected: a vertex array per chunk, and no merged ranges.
Cost and revisit: On an Apple M3 (measured 2026-10-16, vsync off, 1920 x 1200) the grass costs 0 to 0.25 ms of GPU even at the highest density, in either path, which is below the noise of a frame there. So the gain is the work left out (in a corridor about half of the grass triangles, from above the maze about a third), not a faster frame today. The geometry shader path with chunk culling costs about 0.2 ms of CPU per draw call on macOS (the driver emulation), so with culling it can be slower on the CPU than one call without it. The LOD thins the far tufts a little, which is visible only side by side. Native geometry shaders on Windows cards were not measured. I reopen this if the grass gets denser, or if a pass needs base instance (OpenGL 4.2).
